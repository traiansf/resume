Hello,

My name is Traian Șerbănuță and I'm interested in the Senior Software Engineer position.

Nearly all of my academic and industry career has revolved around formal methods, with a particular focus on correct-by-construction programming-language tools (including program verification). This interest began to flourish during my PhD at UIUC, where I co-designed (with Grigore Roșu) and prototyped the K Framework, and continued at Runtime Verification, where I led the effort on K's Haskell backend — a symbolic-execution and program-verification engine used to verify EVM, WebAssembly, and IELE smart contracts. That was complemented by two years of mathematical modelling and proving, with Coq/Rocq, of properties of blockchain consensus protocols stemming from Vlad Zamfir's work with the Ethereum Foundation — including one of the first formalizations of the Casper CBC protocol. For the past two years I've worked as a Rust engineer and researcher at Pi Squared, realizing Grigore Roșu's Proof of Proof vision, including designing and implementing ways to obtain ZK certificates from formal proofs of computation.

In parallel, my academic career at the University of Bucharest has centered on teaching functional programming, programming-language semantics, and program verification.

I wasn't always drawn to functional programming and type systems. My undergraduate training pushed me toward algorithms and competitive programming, and even my PhD work centered on first-order, mostly untyped, rewriting. But as I matured as a software engineer, I came to appreciate FP and to rely on type safety as a way to get closer to the correct-by-construction ideal. Modelling and proving with a proof assistant has been one of the most rewarding experiences of my work, and what drew me to the research around K and matching/reachability logic is that same correct-by-construction conviction. In an ideal world, one shouldn't have to justify a program's correctness after the fact through tests, reviews, audits, and formal verification; instead, both the program and its correctness proof should be generated from a single specification — one clear enough for every interested party to understand.

For the past year, I have leaned heavily on Claude Code in my Rust and TypeScript work. While I greatly appreciate its powers of comprehension, design, and implementation, I'm often dismayed that the best practice for using AI still comes down to TDD and reviews — many of them performed by other AI agents — to "ensure" correctness.

That is why Nectry's mission — to make correctness and policy structurally guaranteed for AI-generated code in regulated settings — is very appealing to me, as is the prospect of working alongside Adam Chlipala, whose research on automatically guaranteeing program correctness I've followed since his work on PHOAS and the Bedrock system. I would be glad to discuss this opportunity further.
